# Abstract

Active retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) triggers external knowledge retrieval during generation based on model-side uncertainty signals to support knowledge-intensive, multi-hop reasoning. However, existing methods often retrieve only after producing a complete answer, failing to surface and fill information gaps in time; moreover, relying on a single internal signal as the trigger cannot adequately capture the multifaceted nature of uncertainty. We therefore propose a conflict-aware active RAG framework. We first decompose complex questions into a sequence of step-level sub-problems. At each step, we quantify local distributional uncertainty via a sliding-window peak token entropy, and estimate cross-sample consensus via the variation ratio computed over multiple Monte Carlo samples. After calibrating both signals onto a probabilistic scale, we quantify their conflict using a symmetric, bounded divergence over Bernoulli parameters, and fuse the three quantities into a single uncertainty score that gates retrieval. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of our framework.

# 1 Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved impressive performance on a wide range of NLP tasks [\[1\]](#page-3-1), but their parametric knowledge is inevitably incomplete and often outdated [\[5\]](#page-3-2). Retrievalaugmented generation (RAG) addresses this limitation by conditioning the model on external documents from a large corpus such as Wikipedia [\[14\]](#page-3-3). For knowledge-intensive, multi-hop question answering (QA), recent work further shows that interleaving retrieval and generation can ground intermediate reasoning steps in retrieved evidence [\[17,](#page-3-4) [18\]](#page-3-5).

A key challenge in RAG is therefore not only how to retrieve, but also when. Classical RAG frameworks such as retrieval-augmented generation [\[8\]](#page-3-6) and FiD [\[6\]](#page-3-7) follow a retrieve–then–generate pipeline, performing a single retrieval pass before decoding the whole answer or using fixed multi-round schedules that are blind to the model's confidence [\[16\]](#page-3-8). More recent active RAG methods make the timing decision by monitoring a single internal signal. For example, FLARE [\[7\]](#page-3-9) tracks token log-probabilities and entropy along the output prefix and triggers sentence-level retrieval when it detects lowconfidence spans, while DRAGIN [\[15\]](#page-3-10) estimates attention-based uncertainty over retrieved passages and dynamically requests more evidence when the attention distribution becomes diffuse. ### 3 Experiments

### 3.1 Experimental Setup

Dataset and backbone. We evaluate on 2WikiMultihopQA [\[3\]](#page-3-18) and HotpotQA [\[17\]](#page-3-4), two multi-hop QA benchmarks in which each question requires combining evidence from at least two Wikipedia articles and is accompanied by annotated reasoning paths. We follow the standard data splits and evaluation protocol, reporting performance on the development set. Unless otherwise stated, all methods use the same Llama2-7b-chat backbone without any additional fine-tuning; our approach is thus training-free with respect to the backbone.

Retrieval and implementation. We follow DRAGIN [\[15\]](#page-3-10) and build a BM25 index over the same English Wikipedia snapshot. For all RAG variants, each retrieval call returns at most �� passages (typically ��=5), which are prepended as a Searched Information block in the prompt. For CFU-RAG, we use the MC decoding and uncertainty estimation described in Section 2.2. We set the number of Monte Carlo samples �� = 5 to balance performance and latency. The fusion weights are fixed to �� = 0.4, �� = 0.4, and �� = 0.2 in the conflict-fused uncertainty score �� , and gating thresholds set to �� = 0.7 and ��both = 0.4. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

Baselines. We compare CFU-RAG against several RAG variants implemented under a unified framework. wo-RAG is vanilla Llama2- 7b-chat used without any retrieval. SR-RAG is a single-round RAG variant that retrieves once given the question and then generates the answer with all retrieved passages at once. FL-RAG is a fixedlength active RAG variant that triggers retrieval after a fixed number of generated tokens. FS-RAG is a fixed-step active RAG variant that triggers retrieval after a fixed number of reasoning steps. FLARE [\[7\]](#page-3-9) is an active RAG variant that uses token-level uncertainty surrogates to decide when to retrieve. DRAGIN [\[15\]](#page-3-10) uses attention-entropy-based gating for dynamic retrieval.

# 3.2 Comparison with SOTA

Table [1](#page-2-0) reports exact match (EM) and token-level F1 on both 2Wiki-MultihopQA and HotpotQA. Overall, all active RAG variants outperform the non-retrieval baseline (wo-RAG) on at least one metric, confirming the benefit of incorporating external evidence. Among the baselines, DRAGIN consistently achieves the strongest performance, improving over single-round (SR-RAG) and fixed-schedule (FL-RAG, FS-RAG) retrieval, and substantially outperforming FLARE, which relies solely on token-level uncertainty surrogates.

<span id="page-3-0"></span>CFU-RAG further improves upon DRAGIN on both datasets, achieving the best EM/F1 under the same backbone. On 2WikiMultihopQA, CFU-RAG yields a noticeable gain over DRAGIN (from 0.224/0.290 to 0.276/0.364 in EM/F1), and on HotpotQA it also improves EM and F1 (from 0.234/0.334 to 0.261/0.352). These consistent gains across datasets suggest that conflict-fused step-wise uncertainty provides a more reliable retrieval trigger than single-view, token-level or attention-based criteria.

Several trends emerge. First, S-only clearly outperforms A-only (EM 0.268 vs. 0.250, F1 0.360 vs. 0.313), indicating that cross-sample disagreement is a stronger standalone indicator of problematic reasoning steps than local token entropy. This matches our intuition that many failure cases arise from confidently wrong but divergent continuations that keep ���� low while producing large variability across MC samples. Second, the Fusion variant achieves the best performance (EM 0.276, F1 0.364), improving over both A-only and S-only. The conflict-fused gate combines the strengths of both views, while the both-high backstop prevents averaging effects from suppressing retrieval when both ���� and ���� are high. # <span id="page-3-10"></span><span id="page-3-8"></span><span id="page-3-3"></span>4 Conclusion

<span id="page-3-5"></span><span id="page-3-4"></span>We studied when to trigger retrieval in active RAG for multi-hop QA. Existing methods mainly rely on a single, token-level uncertainty signal and often miss confidently wrong intermediate steps. We proposed CFU-RAG, a training-free framework that operates at the step level and fuses local entropy, cross-sample disagreement, and their conflict into a single uncertainty score for gating retrieval. In experiments on 2WikiMultihopQA and HotpotQA with a shared

Llama2-7b-chat backbone and BM25 retriever, CFU-RAG consistently improves EM/F1 over strong RAG baselines, and ablations show that conflict-fused uncertainty yields more reliable gating than either uncertainty view alone.
