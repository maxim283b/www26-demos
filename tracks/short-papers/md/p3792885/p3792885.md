# Abstract

<span id="page-0-0"></span>

We introduce Reverse–Mutual Reasoning for Mathematical Thinking (ReMi-ReMath), a mutual reasoning framework that enhances mathematical reasoning in small language models without requiring fine-tuning. Building on the mutual reasoning structure of rStar, ReMi-ReMath expands it by incorporating backward reasoningbased discriminative reconstruction and a self-evaluation system that measures logical and semantic consistency, rather than simple answer matching. Across benchmarks, ReMi-ReMath improves performance by 3–5%p over rStar using 3.8B-scale models, demonstrating that combining mutual reasoning and reverse reconstruction establishes a new paradigm for quantitatively verifying reasoning validity beyond answer-centric evaluations.

# 1 Introduction

Recently, Large Language Models (LLMs) show remarkable achievements in mathematical problem solving (Math Reasoning) by mimicking human 'System 2' thinking (slow and logical thought processes). However, these achievements are mostly the result of dependence on large-scale parameters and supervised data (fine-tuning data), and small language models (SLMs) still show limitations in effectively exploring complex reasoning spaces and maintaining logical consistency [\[5\]](#page-3-1).

To overcome these limitations, prior research [\[2\]](#page-3-2) proposed the rStar (Self-play Mutual Reasoning) framework. rStar adopted a structure in which two models share roles as generators and discriminators to mutually verify the reasoning process (mutual verification) to improve SLMs' thinking capabilities without large-scale teacher models. Specifically, rStar generates various reasoning trajectories based on the Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) and induces mutual consistency by having another SLM partially complete and verify them. This peer-level mutual reasoning is significant because it presents a new direction for improving reasoning quality without supervised data. However, rStar's approach still remained focused on forward-centered answer mutual agreement. Although the generated reasoning paths could reach mutually consistent conclusions, they did not directly verify whether the process satisfied the premise of the actual problem or whether the answer was logically derivable (logical entailment). What rStar solved was "consensus on answer," but "consistency of reasoning" was still difficult to measure.

This leads SLMs to still tend to select "logically inconsistent reasoning that has correct answers." As mentioned in the rStar paper, this phenomenon stems from (1) the problem of SLM's self-exploration remaining in low-quality reasoning spaces and (2) the absence of clear verification criteria to discriminate reasoning quality [\[3\]](#page-3-3). Ultimately, it is difficult to determine whether the model has internalized logical validity based on answer-centric evaluation alone. This limitation suggests a stage that must be overcome for small models to evolve from "models that get correct answers" to "models that understand and verify logic."

rStar [\[2\]](#page-3-2) demonstrated the possibility of improving thinking quality without supervised data for SLMs. However, its approach was essentially limited to answer-centric mutual reasoning. While the two models could compare each other's reasoning and reach consistent conclusions (consistency on answer), there was no system to verify whether that reasoning actually satisfied the problem's

Humans do not stop at getting results right but question whether the obtained conclusion is logically entailed from the given conditions. In other words, reason validity is more important than answer consistency. However, existing mutual reasoning frameworks cannot perform this level of verification.

We propose a new framework, Reverse–Mutual Reasoning for Mathematical Thinking (ReMi-ReMath), which extends the concept of mutual reasoning to logical verification tasks. This inherits the mutual verification structure proposed by rStar [\[2\]](#page-3-2), while we utilize 'reason validity' rather than 'consistency of answer' as the verification target. ReMi-ReMath combines Forward Reasoning and Backward Verification to evaluate the tri-consistency between problem and reasoning–answer throughout the reasoning process as its core goal. The main contributions are as follows: 1) proposal of a reverse-mutual reasoning framework; 2) establishment of a logicsemantic consistency-based self-evaluation structure; 3) zero-shot self-verification operation without fine-tuning; 4) tri-consistencybased evaluation metrics and experimental verification.

ReMi-ReMath transforms existing mutual reasoning from "answercentered peer agreement" to "logic-centered self-verification." Ultimately, this study evolves models beyond simply reaching correct answers to understand and verify logic, fundamentally improving the reasoning reliability of SLMs. Our code is available at [https://github.com/deepvine/ReMi-ReMath.](https://github.com/deepvine/ReMi-ReMath)

#### 2 Method

ReMi-ReMath has an integrated forward–reverse structure centered on a Mutual Reasoning Discriminator (MRD). This is an extension of the Self-Verifying Mutual Reasoning paradigm proposed by rStar [\[2\]](#page-3-2) to the mathematical reasoning domain, designed to enable models to logically evaluate their reasoning quality within single-pass verification without additional fine-tuning. The core idea of ReMi-ReMath is to replace answer-centric (peer agreement) evaluation with logic-semantic consistency-based evaluation. For this, the model performs a three-stage reverse mutual Reasoning Pipeline consisting of 'forward generation–reverse reconstruction–mutual verification' (Figure [1\)](#page-0-0). The process consists of three stages:

- (1) Forward Generation: Generator generates various reasoning paths from problems.
- (2) Reverse Reconstruction: MRD receives each reasoning and final answer as input and reverse restores problems that the reasoning could be valid for.
- (3) Dual-Axis Verification: We evaluate reasoning validity by comparing numeric-semantic consistency between reconstructed and original problems.

Through this process, ReMi-ReMath performs self-verification of reasoning quality based on logic–semantic consistency beyond simple peer agreement.

### 2.1 Forward Generation

In the first stage, Forward Generation, an SLM-based Generator is used to produce multiple reasoning trajectories. This stage goes beyond finding correct answers to secure rich reasoning spaces for subsequent Reverse Reconstruction by diversely exploring logically complete paths.

2.1.1 Monte Carlo Tree Search-based Exploration. To ensure exploration diversity, a stochastic exploration strategy combining each node's visit count and reward estimation is applied [\[1\]](#page-3-4). Through this, different reasoning paths, such as the direct calculation and condition decomposition types, are naturally explored from the same problem, and various thought paths can be secured without being trapped in local optima. Meanwhile, to maintain computational efficiency, ReMi-ReMath limits the search depth (��) within a predefined maximum depth, considering sLLM environment constraints. This design achieves a balance between exploration diversity and computational efficiency by securing sufficient reasoning candidates, even with computational complexity levels.

2.1.2 Step-wise Reasoning Unit. Each path consists of a series of explicit reasoning steps composed of intermediate calculations, assumptions, logical transitions, etc., starting from the problem conditions (premise). Models receive previous step output as context to generate next step, and all steps follow the structure of "understanding problem conditions → intermediate reasoning results → conclusion." This hierarchical reasoning structure induces models to learn logical transitions across sentences beyond single-sentencelevel calculations, ultimately contributing to the formation of more consistent mathematical thinking systems.

2.1.3 Candidate Filtering. The generated trajectory set is collected via exploration (rollout) processes using an MCTS-based self-generator. Each trajectory represents a series of reasoning paths where models reach final conclusions following intermediate reasoning processes step-by-step, starting from problem conditions. ReMi-ReMath inherits rStar's generator structure as is, maintaining all trajectories obtained from rollouts as candidates without prior filtering. In subsequent verification stages, the MRD evaluates the logical consistency and conclusion validity of each trajectory through mutual verification, ultimately selecting reliable reasoning trajectories.

# 2.2 Mutual Reasoning Discriminator (MRD)

#### 3 Experiments

All comparisons utilized the same backbone (phi-3-mini), scale (parameter count, tokenizer, context length), and data splits as previous studies. Benchmarks employed official GSM8K, GSM8K-HARD, and MATH test sets, generating a single numerical answer per sample and calculating Accuracy via exact matching. Standardized answer preprocessing was applied: numerical strings were stripped of spaces, separators, and currency symbols; fractions, decimals, and percentages were consistently formatted; and problem-specific rounding rules were prioritized.

#### 3.1 Main Results

ReMi-ReMath is tested using several mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, GSM8K-HARD, MATH, and SVAMP). The comparison targets consisted of rStar [\[2\]](#page-3-2) and our model, using Phi-3 mini-4k-instruct backbone. All models are evaluated with identical decoding hyperparameters (temperature=0.7, top-p=0.9, maxtokens=512), generating one final numerical answer per problem.

The proposed ReMi-ReMath records a higher accuracy than rStar across all benchmarks. This implies that even under identical forward exploration structures, the proposed generator forms more consistent thought paths. Meanwhile, our method, including the reverse reconstruction-based verification module, achieves the highest performance across all three benchmarks. In particular, improvement margins are prominent in GSM8K-HARD and MATH, which require complex operations and long-range logic. These results show that structures evaluating the tri-consistency between problem-reasoning-answer rather than simple peer agreement actually lead to reasoning quality improvement. ### 4 Concluding Remarks

ReMi-ReMath extends mutual reasoning to reverse reasoning-based logical verification by evaluating tri-consistency between problems, reasoning, and answers through forward-reverse reconstruction. Experiments demonstrate 3-5%p accuracy improvements over rStar across multiple benchmarks using only 3.8B-scale models. Future work includes lightweight pruning, hybrid verification, and generalization to larger models.
